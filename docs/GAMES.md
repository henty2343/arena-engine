### Fighter skill vs weapon trait — the model (owner ruling 2026-10-28: **M2**, two languages — **the first special skill BUILT as `skill-r29` and RETIRED at `nocombo-r34`**; see NO COMBOS below)

**The ruling.** A weapon's **trait** (the row field was `ability` until the 2026-10-28 rename below) is _data_ — a growth rate on the shared axes, read at the one site where growth happens (`grow()`). A fighter's **skill** is a **hook** in `CONTRACT.md`'s sense: it reads the player and a small context and returns a modified value, never mutates state, never depends on another player's hook, **modifies an existing mechanic and never introduces one**, may consume the game's seeded RNG but never create or seed one, and leaves anything time-dependent to the game's own per-tick logic. One skill per player per game, expressed through one hook or several; identity stays global, skill stays local.

**Adopted lazily — this ruling changes no code.** CONTRACT's optionality rule (where a game calls a hook a player doesn't implement, the base value passes through unchanged) means the two shipped numeric skills — `Speedy {spin: 0.30}`, `Heavy {dmg: 0.9}` — stay legal exactly as they are, and the hook indirection is introduced by the **first non-numeric skill**, not before. Nothing is threaded speculatively; the canonical is untouched by this ruling (md5 `86ff2d5bffc6a3942e6c9eba12ccef09`, build `sigil-r23`).

**The consequence that shapes the pass.** Because a hook may not introduce a mechanic, anything a skill is meant to touch must exist in the _game_ first: poison, frost, comet burn, damage-over-time and runtime length growth are **game-layer mechanics**, and only then can a skill modify them. The pass has two layers — **(1) authorized hook targets** (game mechanics, each owing its own probe) and **(2) the hooks themselves**.

**Why M2 and not M1 — the measured state of the one-language build.** `grow()` is the _only_ sim consumer of `.skill` and `.trait`. Traits are binary across all 22 weapons (12 × `{spin: 0, dmg: 0.7}`, 10 × `{spin: 0.70, dmg: 0}`), so the unit of differentiation today is the _(fighter × weapon)_ pair resolving to one of four growth vectors — the measured ω 103.5 maximum is exactly the Speedy × spin-weapon cell (+1.00/hit × 100 hits). The display hard-codes that binary: `SKILL_SHORT = f.skill.spin ? '+SPD' : '+DMG'`, so a fighter with both axes, neither, or a non-numeric skill is **silently mislabelled "+DMG / Hit"**; the ridiculousness trackers are exactly two (`G.maxSpin`, `G.maxDmg`) against a fixed three-channel SPD-DMG legend; and a shot inherits exactly one per-fighter quantity at birth (`dmg: f.dmg`), with `r`/`speed`/`len`/`turn`/`bounce` all reading the weapon row — which is what makes per-fighter shot axes the cheap spectacle (one line each at the birth site).

**Cost of any new axis** (the budget rule, from the skills-prep cost map): a fighter field plus its per-round reset · the consumer site that reads it at hit or launch time · the legend/poster, where a fourth channel is a layout change · a `maxDmg`/`maxSpin`-class tracker · the fingerprint's hashed fields · **and its own probe** — the facing pass's vacuous-unextended-run trap is the precedent: a harness that passes without covering the new flag proves nothing.

**Closed alongside it (owner pick 2026-10-28): parry rewards nothing, full stop — and no skill may reward a parry either.** That removes spin-per-parry, which the spin-ceiling measurement named the likeliest way to wake the dormant `omegaCap: 140`, and leaves **damaging hit, time and launch** as the only growth triggers. Time and launch are contextual, so either one is the first thing that forces a real hook rather than a number.

**Settled under M2 — the five rulings (owner, 2026-10-28).** **(1) Stacking: it adds.** A fighter's skill and the weapon's trait stack additively on the shared axes, exactly as `grow()` already does (`sk.spin + ab.spin`, `sk.dmg + ab.dmg`), so the numeric site is unchanged by the model ruling; a hook that modifies a _different_ mechanic adds to the growth the weapon contributes rather than replacing it. **(2) Display: shown in the legend, as a short authored label.** The legend already carries a skill cell per fighter (the `class="ex"` cell between the name and the weapon columns), so this is an **authoring change, not a layout change**:
`SKILL_SHORT`'s binary derivation (`f.skill.spin ? '+SPD' : '+DMG'`) is replaced by a per-fighter few-word line in the style of the existing poster prose (`line: 'Spins faster each hit'`) — the owner's example for a spear: **"gets longer"**. Two consequences: the silent-mislabel defect dies with the derivation (a both-axes / neither / non-numeric skill can no longer read "+DMG / Hit"), and the weapon column keeps its own `brief` + `line`. The SPD/DMG numeric cell is untouched — **a new _numeric_ axis is still a layout change; a prose label is free.** **(3) Authorized hook targets: yes — length growth, per-fighter shot multipliers and knock.** Each is game-layer work first (a hook may not introduce a mechanic) and each owes its own probe.
Length growth stays the expensive one: `W.len` is consumed at boot to build SPAN, REACH, the scaled polys and the baked covering circles, so runtime growth threads a per-fighter length scale through `spineFrame`, `bladeCircles` and the renderer, invalidates REACH (the parryGap prune bound) and MINR (the adaptive-substep budget), and cannot be caught by the boot LEN*MIN/LT gate — **the ceiling has to live inside the growth rule**. **NO COMBOS (owner ruling 2026-10-31, codified at `nocombo-r34`).** A skill and a trait may stack **only by addition on a shared axis**; neither may reach into the other's mechanic. There are **no unique fighter×weapon combinations**: one bot's weapon may never behave differently from anyone else's, and every skill must be **universal** (live on all 22 weapons) or **contextual in a class-independent way**, so a bot's identity survives the dice roll by construction. **Axis placement (owner ruling 2026-10-28: "design it out").** An axis whose effect depends on the weapon **class** is carried as **weapon-row data** — a rate, exactly like `trait: {spin, dmg}` — and consumed by a game-layer mechanic: the launch-snapshot axes (`r`/`speed`/`len`/`turn`/`bounce`, live on the 10 projectile weapons) and runtime length growth (live on the 12 melee ones). A **fighter's skill** takes only what is live on all 22 weapons — spin growth, damage growth, knock (`CFG.hitKnock`, whose two consumers are `bladeVsBody` and `shotHitBody`) — or a contextual trigger that does not depend on weapon class — the class-independent contextual triggers are per-time and conditional-on-state only — **per-launch is class-dependent** (only the 10 projectile weapons ever launch), so a launch-triggered effect belongs on the weapon row, not on a fighter.
**Why:** weapons are dice-rolled each round, so a fighter's identity has to survive the roll; a class-dependent fighter skill flickers (live on 12/22 or 10/22), while a weapon-keyed rate never does, because its line is only ever read while that weapon is held. **Consequence for display: no dark label can exist**, so the legend needs no conditional or dimmed state and this ruling owes **no chrome work** — the skill cell becomes the authored few-word line of ruling 2 and is always true, and the class-dependent flavour rides in the weapon's own `line`/`brief`. **M2 stays intact:** a rate on the weapon row is still _data_, the mechanic consuming it is still game-layer, and only the fighter's skill is a hook.
**What it re-keys:** the length-growth spectacle ("gets longer") belongs to the **spear** — and to any other melee weapon given a length rate — not to a fighter; either fighter gets it whenever they hold that weapon, and it is still built in this pass. **Rejected with reasons** (so the fork is not re-litigated): conditional label wording ("longer reaches when melee") — free, but the label sits dark on half the rolls and the audience cannot tell why nothing happened; dimming the label while dark — cheap and idiomatic (the legend already carries `class="out"` for a dead fighter and `wp idle` + "rolling…" pre-reveal, it re-renders per frame and already resolves the held weapon, and chromecheck asserts legend _structure_ only — `ccard`, `table`, `qn` — so it would cost one CSS rule, a re-run and the same treatment on the VS poster), but unnecessary once no dark label can exist. Both remain available if a specific skill is ever judged worth the flicker.

### Length growth — the spear's trait (owner ruling 2026-10-28, **BUILT as `lengrow-r26`, raised to +35% as `lengrow-r27`, then to +200% as `lengrow-r28` — all the same day** — rigid head, junction at 69.23, per-step RE-BAKED coverings)

**The proposal, and why it is a confirmation rather than a new direction.** Owner, 2026-10-28: _"ok, so i was thinking the spear should grow longer each time it hits an opponent, what do you think"_. Reach goes 205.9 → **~228px** (61.44px radial grip + 144.5px covering extent × 1.154). Roughly one visible step per clean hit, topping out near the end of a long round. **Amended 2026-10-28, the same day, on the owner's _"i want it to be more ridiculous, so increase the growth of the spear even more"_: the rate is now `len: 0.0253` — 2.5% per damaging hit, 12 steps to **+34.96%** — build `lengrow-r27`.** The sizing logic above holds and was re-run rather than assumed:

**No `lenCap` constant is introduced — the round IS the cap.** Owner, 2026-10-28: _"no cap let it snowball, we will balance laer if it gets too ridiculous"_. That is safe here for a structural reason: **stats reset every round** (the "fresh snowball" ruling), so growth can never compound across a battle, and a round delivers only ~14 growth events. Stalled rounds are self-limiting too — growth requires a damaging hit, and a stall has few. The 12th step is therefore a **technical** ceiling set by the hitbox, not a gameplay cap; the later balancing conversation is about the _rate_, not the ceiling.
Note this is a genuine snowball unlike the other two axes: length raises the _contact area_, which raises the hit rate, which raises the growth rate — superlinear, where spin and damage growth are linear. That is the reason a bound was worth finding even though none was mandated.

**Length-only, NOT uniform — the owner's stated fear, and the reason the obvious implementation was rejected.** Owner: _"does uniform mean on ll sides? i was actually preferring only the lenght, im scared the spear will become "thick""_. It does: a uniform scale multiplies `d` (along the spine), `l` (across it) and `r` (the circle radius) together, so the spear would visibly fatten as it grew. **Rejected.** The chosen geometry stretches **`d` only, holding `l` and `r`** — the shaft elongates and nothing thickens.

**The measured ceiling on length-only growth.** Stretching `d` alone spreads the baked covering circles apart along the spine while their radii stay fixed, so past a per-weapon limit the union stops covering the drawn silhouette and a body inside the art can fail to take damage — precisely the _"gap hits"_ the owner made top priority on 2026-10-19. | sword     | +53.9%                                                         |
| hammer    | +28.2%                                                         |

At the chosen 12 × 1.2% the design sits at +15.4%, inside even the proxy.

**The trilemma, and how the per-round reset dissolves it.** _Length-only_, _no cap_ and _no gap hits_ cannot all hold: length-only + no gap hits is bounded; length-only + no cap produces gap hits past ~+18%; no cap + no gap hits requires a uniform scale, i.e. thickening. ### Movement speed — cruise, spikes and settle (doctrine + build 2026-10-21 — REVERTED same day at owner playtest; design record only)

**REVERTED at owner playtest (2026-10-21, owner: "ok i dont like that at all, it does not look natural anymore like it did before").** The sim is back to pre-doctrine ballistics, proven by fingerprint byte-identical to `.qa/fp-pre-cruise.json`. The doctrine is normative for the build; the mechanics stay as shipped until then.

1. **Cruise is a floor, never a steer.** Every living fighter in the fight phase holds |v| ≥ cruise. The floor scales magnitude along the current travel direction only; at near-zero speed the fallback direction is gravity's. The regulator never adds lateral velocity: **direction is authored by gravity and contact alone** (walls, floor, bodies, blades) — the owner's falling-straight-down prohibition, written as a rule.
2. **Ballistics stay ballistics.** Gravity's own speed authorship — accelerating falls, slow apexes, the floaty arc that "looks good right now" — is never damped, and the round's launch speed (430) remains the opening ballistic baseline. Settle never touches gravity-authored speed; it only drains collision-authored excess.
3. **Only collisions author excess; excess settles.** Parry shove, hit knock, body-body exchange and the floor trampoline's injection add speed above the ballistic baseline; that excess decays with the settle half-life back toward the baseline, so a sped-up bot visibly calms (~12% of the excess left after 1.5s) and nothing ratchets forever. Today nothing subtracts speed — elastic walls preserve it, injections only add — so late-round pace creeps toward the temporary cap structurally; settle is the fix. Walls stay perfectly elastic (they preserve the arc energy that makes the current look); the floor re-injects its spike at every touch, so the perpetual bounce rhythm stays the show — now with a settling peak instead of a permanent one.
4. **Scope:** fight phase, living fighters only. Reveal, K.O. and over poses are untouched; the dead stay frozen as today.
5. **Spin doctrine stands as built:** shared ω 3.5, growth on damaging hits from fighter skill AND weapon trait — re-confirmed 2026-10-21 as balance-pass material, not a doctrine change (owner: "dont bother its going to be part of balancing later").
6. **Vocabulary (SPEC one-word rule):** **cruise speed** = the shared default floor; **excess** = speed above the ballistic baseline; **settle** = the excess's decay. No synonyms in docs, UI or harness output.
7. **Dial rationale (assistant, viewer-first, recorded for the balance pass):** cruise 250 — low enough that typical apex breathing (|vx| ≈ 250–430) survives as the approved floaty arc, high enough that the crawl/hover states (apex with little horizontal, bottom-centre cradle dwells — the stall reds' habitat) become a gentle glide: no dead air on the frame. **Launch doctrine (owner 2026-10-19):** "when the weapon is radial, shoot from the tip, when it is the opposite, shoot from the middle, like bow and crossbow." ## Design principles for all games

- **Structure before flavour.** Boredom is fixed by mechanics that generate swings, not by scheduled events or character complexity.
- **Reversals must be attributable.** A swing should be caused by something the audience can point at — a contact, a capture, a raid — never by a hidden number or a scheduled event. (The dice roll is exempt by design: it is an _initial condition_, shown openly before the round, never a mid-fight swerve.)
- **Quiet frame, loud fight.** The owner's settled taste across every visual round: warm, clean, undistracting chrome; all motion and color budget spent inside the arena. Effects never obscure match state; ridiculousness comes from mechanics, not decoration.
- **Hitboxes are the contract with the viewer.** The audience's trust rests on "what you see is what hits": the drawn silhouette _is_ the collision shape, every registered contact is provable on the frame it's shown, and solids never silently pass through solids. Owner, 2026-10-16: "for the viewer perfect hitboxes is the most important." SPINCLASH's **Core contact law** is this principle written as rules; a headless audit re-verifies it after every change. It ranks above visual polish — a beautiful frame that lies about contact is a broken frame.
- **Fundamentals before furniture.** Settle the mechanics contract (what damages, what collides, what is solid) before investing in anything drawn on top of it — owner, 2026-10-16: "we need to get the fundamental mechanics right before going further with the weapons." Weapons get redesigned repeatedly; the contact law they must obey does not. (Distinct from _Structure before flavour_, which is about what cures boredom.)
