### Fighter skill vs weapon trait — the model (**M2**, two languages — **the first special skill BUILT and then RETIRED**; see NO COMBOS below)

**The ruling.** A weapon's **trait** (the row field was `ability` until the rename below) is _data_ —
a growth rate on the shared axes, read at the one site where growth happens (`grow`).
A fighter's **skill** is a **hook** in `docs/planned/CONTRACT.md`'s sense:
it reads the player and a small context and returns a modified value,
never mutates state, never depends on another player's hook,
**modifies an existing mechanic and never introduces one**,
may consume the game's seeded RNG but never create or seed one,
and leaves anything time-dependent to the game's own per-tick logic.
One skill per player per game, expressed through one hook or several;
identity stays global, skill stays local.

**Adopted lazily — this ruling changes no code.** CONTRACT's optionality rule (where a game calls a hook a player doesn't implement, the base value passes through unchanged) means the two shipped numeric skills —
`Speedy {spin: 0.30}`, `Heavy {dmg: 0.9}` —
stay legal exactly as they are, and the hook indirection is introduced by the **first non-numeric skill**, not before. Nothing is threaded speculatively;
the canonical is untouched by this ruling — its md5 and build stamp live in `STATE.md`.

**The consequence that shapes the pass.** Because a hook may not introduce a mechanic, anything a skill is meant to touch must exist in the _game_ first:
poison, frost, comet burn, damage-over-time and runtime length growth are **game-layer mechanics**, and only then can a skill modify them.
The pass has two layers — **(1) authorized hook targets** (game mechanics, each owing its own probe) and **(2) the hooks themselves**.

**Why M2 and not M1 — the measured state of the one-language build.** `grow` is the _only_ sim consumer of `.skill` and `.trait`.
Traits are binary across all 22 weapons (12 × `{spin: 0, dmg: 0.7}`, 10 × `{spin: 0.70, dmg: 0}`), so the unit of differentiation today is the _(fighter × weapon)_ pair resolving to one of four growth vectors —
the measured ω 103.5 maximum is exactly the Speedy × spin-weapon cell (+1.00/hit × 100 hits). The display hard-codes that binary:
`SKILL_SHORT = f.skill.spin? '+SPD': '+DMG'`, so a fighter with both axes, neither, or a non-numeric skill is **silently mislabelled "+DMG / Hit"**;
the ridiculousness trackers are exactly two (`G.maxSpin`, `G.maxDmg`) against a fixed three-channel SPD-DMG legend;

**The projectile rule — RETIRED at `allcut-r62`:** the rule no longer holds.
EVERY weapon's spine cuts on body touch — the ten launchers AND the shield —
at the WDEF base damage, feeding grow like any melee hit. The spine stays a full solid:
it latches and knocks the body, parries weapon-vs-weapon, and swats enemy shots.
The projectile remains the launcher's growth ENGINE but is no longer its only damage.

**Firing is physics, not a scheduler.** One shot leaves per full revolution of the spin (a per-fighter radian accumulator, `fireAcc += |spin|·sdt`, releases at 2π) —
**spin rate IS fire rate**, no cooldown stat, no ammo, no new vocabulary:
`+SPD / Hit` on a launcher _is_ faster shooting.
Firing falls out of the physics because nobody pilots these bots.

**Launch doctrine:** A **radial-hold** launcher releases at the spine **tip** (t = 1), flying outward along the spine (staff, wand).
A **tangential-hold** launcher releases at the spine **middle** (t = 0.5, the grip point on the rim); there the direction splits per weapon:
_shooting_ weapons (bow, crossbow — `fire: 'radial'`) send the shot radially **out** of the grip, offset by `shot.off` so the head starts at the belly —
a bow shoots where its belly faces, not along its limbs; _thrown_ weapons (boomerang, shuriken) leave **tangentially in the spin direction**.
Law 6s recomputes every launch from its stored firing pose to the hold-derived point (0 off-point ever, 6175 checked at the v1.2 probe).
This.7.2 "always from the middle" rule.

**Shot damage:** the shot launches with the thrower's **current** damage — snapshotted at launch —
and there is no separate projectile stat (one damage number per fighter, `a.dmg`; it grows on hit via the fighter's skill, so a Heavy's shots fatten +0.9 per hit while a Speedy's stay light and come faster).

**Shot flight and deaths.** Straight flight, no gravity, no drag —
the boomerang alone curves (its locked 421×250px oval, row turn 4.0 with the 0.65 elongation modulation, anchored to its wielder — `halo-r39`).
Deaths, in the fixed deterministic per-substep order rim → body → swat → shot-shot:
**rim** kills (law 1 — walls never damage); the sanctioned exceptions are the shuriken star's **ETERNAL RICOCHET** (`shot.ricochet`, `ricolaw-r41` — the rim never kills a star; it mirrors forever at row speed), the boomerang's **pierce**,
and the crossbow's **NAIL** (`shot.pin`, `pinlaw-r44` — the bolt sticks just inside the rim as a shot AT REST, `speed` 0, keeping its impact flight line as the drawn facing (`pinfix-r45`; the inward knock vector is stored separately as `pnx/pny`); it ends only on the foe's body (the law-4 hit, consumed), the foe's blade (swat), an in-flight enemy shot (cross) or the roll-clear; the walks through own nails; nail×nail never crosses — statics cannot visibly resolve).
Shot-vs-shot is ENEMY-ONLY since `multgrow-r42` (generalizing the r41 star exemption at the option-A ruling):
SAME-OWNER pairs skip the cross test on EVERY shot class —
friendly fire does not exist; enemy pairs still cancel both.
**Body** contact is a damaging hit and consumes the shot (law 4 structural — the fourth row-owned exception is the javelin's **SKEWER**, `skewer-r46`: the hit does NOT consume the spear; it impales, carries the victim to the rim, pins and bleeds them, and only the victim's own blade cutting it, an enemy cross, or the roll ends it; a killing hit consumes normally).
**Blade** contact is a **swat**: the shot is destroyed and nothing else happens —
no parry event, **no spin flip on either fighter** (the named, documented rule-5 exception; readable on the reveal poster and legend line).
**Shot-vs-shot** cancels both — enemy pairs only (symmetric, provable).
**No friendly fire** — built as _owner-blindness_: a shot is never tested against its body, blade or SHOTS (`multgrow-r42`), so it can never self-destruct, self-swat or self-cross.
**Law 3 turns asymmetric:** a shot has no body behind it to push, so instead of being separated it **loses every collision it makes** —
never overlapping blade, body, rim or another shot on a rendered frame.
Detection keeps the same guarantee as blades: substeps sized so relative travel per substep ≤ half the narrowest window —
blade-tip vs shot speed is the largest relative velocity in the sim, and since v1.2 the shot-vs-shot term budgets the **worst pair** of in-flight shots (mixed speeds: spark 800 vs orb 430 — the old single-speed formula under-budgeted);
the audit checks the sim's chosen k against both budgets every tick.

**Drawn shape vs hitbox.** Disc-shaped shots (orb, spark, star, boomerang) draw at exactly `shot.r` — the drawn disc _is_ the hitbox.
Arrows and bolts (`shot.len`) draw shaft + fletching **trailing behind** the hitbox circle, which sits at the head —
the business end is what hits, the trail is paint. "What you see is what hits" survives with that stated reading.

## Design principles for all games

**Structure before flavour.** Boredom is fixed by mechanics that generate swings, not by scheduled events or character complexity.
**Reversals must be attributable.** A swing should be caused by something the audience can point at — a contact, a capture, a raid —
never by a hidden number or a scheduled event.
(The dice roll is exempt by design: it is an _initial condition_, shown openly before the round, never a mid-fight swerve.)
**Quiet frame, loud fight.** The settled taste across every visual round: warm, clean, undistracting chrome; all motion and color budget spent inside the arena. Effects never obscure match state; ridiculousness comes from mechanics, not decoration.
**Hitboxes are the contract with the viewer.** The audience's trust rests on:
the drawn silhouette _is_ the collision shape, every registered contact is provable on the frame it's shown, and solids never silently pass through solids.,
SPINCLASH's **Core contact law** is this principle written as rules;
a headless audit re-verifies it after every change. It ranks above visual polish —
a beautiful frame that lies about contact is a broken frame.
**Fundamentals before furniture.** Settle the mechanics contract (what damages, what collides, what is solid) before investing in anything drawn on top of it —,
Weapons get redesigned repeatedly;
the contact law they must obey does not.
(Distinct from _Structure before flavour_, which is about what cures boredom.)
